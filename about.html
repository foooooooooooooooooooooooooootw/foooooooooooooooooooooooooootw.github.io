Chapter One

Cosmology

Nominative DeterminismBlood Memory

Ethnically, most of my ancestors come from South China. Although I may not look like it, Nanyang is where my family has been for generations. My birth cert reads "Hainanese", but that discounts the rest of my bloodline which includes cantonese, teochew and even a tiny bit of persian.

From a religious standpoint, I was raised Methodist. Most of my nuclear family converted spontaneously to Christianity at different times, and to different denominations.

Growing up, I was steeped in tales of my family’s history – my great-grandfather a warlord, my grandfather a volunteer soldier, and so on. It seemed my lineage was marked by a recurring need to take up arms, long before conscription became commonplace. I find myself grateful for the relative peace of my time, though I suspect we are on the brink of a world war.

Strange name? Long story. Well, by now people have called me everything; my nicknames include Shen, FT, Foo, Shay, etc the list goes on.

Feel free to just call me Foo if it's easier for you since I have no english/christian name; it's how most of my foreign friends address me.

My chinese name is

  • 符 [Talisman/Seal/Symbol]
  • 传 [Pass on/Spread/Transmit/Conduct/Summon]
  • 晖 [Sunlight]

Chapter Two

Zero Hour

Base · SingaporeBorn · 1997

Born to a family of lawyers (which goes about as well as you'd expect) meant most interactions, regardless if I was the witness or interlocutor, were cross-examinations. I'm not saying it was bad though, brutal as it was at times. The one phrase my parents would always say was, "Look at a situation from every angle."

I was fortunate enough to grow up surrounded by nature. As a child I would be brought into either the clementi forest or the bukit timah forest depending on who I was with. My family dog Bonnie would also kept me company from the crib till I could stand on my two feet.

In my childhood I was often found with a screwdriver in hand, taking apart anything that caught my attention; sometimes the opposite was true - I'd combine things in a frankenstein manner that didn't make sense. Inevitably, I gravitated towards engineering in my teenage years, though that was just the beginning of my journey.

Worlds end. And when yours does, make sure you have a partner you can rely on.

Chapter three

Liftoff

AlienEruditionDisillusionment

I felt a constant sense of being an outsider during my teenage years – different in so many ways, not just in how I spoke or looked, but in my interests. I’d been studying business, and my English had improved dramatically, with ambitions of pursuing a business or arts degree. But again, my parents quickly intervened, urging me towards a STEM field, which led me to Marine Engineering, given my location in a port city. I enjoyed the work, but the idea of being at sea for months at a time really put me off.

Soon after, I found myself in the military as a conscript. When I finished my basic military training, there was a newfound bitterness in my heart. I hadn’t yet gained the right to vote, yet I was being trained to kill and die for my country. I saw my brothers-in-arms being treated poorly, shouted at, and pushed to their limits, conditioned to take orders and throw themselves into gunfire. This would go on to alter my perception on a lot of things.

Later on, I would seek to switch fields. Computers were my hobby, and I had programmed from a young age. As such, I wanted to formalize it and get a degree. Fortunately, James Cook University would take me in and give me a substantial grant which I am very grateful for. Although my time there was uneventful since I was already adept at IT before even stepping foot into the school, I was granted the opportunity to make friends with many international students. Many of the friends I made there were from Myanmar which gave me insight into the terrible happenings in their country, alongside the intricacies and delicacies their culture had to offer. If you've never tried food from myanmar, I must recommend fried roselle salad.

Chapter four

Interstellar

Sans FrontièresExploring New Worlds

After graduating from the IT degree, I sought again to become even more... erudite. The basic degree was wide, in that it covered everything from programming to content management and data mining. After jumping through numerous hoops and taking the GRE, I would be admitted into Singapore Management University, under the MITB (FTA) Masters degree. Supposedly, its the 2nd best in Asia under Global Master's in Business Analytics. I'd entered this postgraduate degree at about 25 years old, which meant my cohort all had years or even decades of experience under their belt. And while the rigor of the syllabus was definitely unparalleled insofar as I'd come across, I learnt a lot from the friends I had made.

Most of my classmates were from other countries. In my undergraduate I got to meet people from Myanmar, Cambodia and Kazakhstan, while in my postgraduate I got to meet peers from Taiwan, China, India, Mongolia, Myanmar (again), Vietnam and Philippines. My world view was rapidly expanding, and to my luck I had opportunities to have deep talks with some of them about how we perceived our countries.

What is a nation? Show me one.

Chapter five

Current Coordinates

Ergo Sum

I'm anti-war and anti-military. Most people in Asia face mandatory conscription, and I refuse to fight a war against my brothers overseas - regardless of race or religion. This shared view has allowed me to quickly form strong bonds with any conscript I've met in Asia.

People have often described me as having ‘the patience of a saint,’ perhaps with a touch of concern about a ‘savior complex.’ I genuinely believe that when someone asks for help, it’s a serious matter that deserves a response, and I feel a responsibility to see things through to the end.

I'm also pro-consumer, typically right to repair, and generally harbour a strong resentment towards consumerism. If it's any indicator, my phone is 6 years old and has undergone a screen replacement, 2 battery replacements, a rear camera replacement, charging port replacement, side button fixes and a few fingerprint scanner replacements. All carried out by yours truly of course.
